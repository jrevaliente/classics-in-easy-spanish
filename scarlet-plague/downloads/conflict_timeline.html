Story & Conflict Timeline

Spoilers for all six chapters. Review after finishing the story.

Keep the time frames separate

The present-time frame takes place around an old railway and a beach once visited by crowds from San Francisco. The book begins with a bear, a silver coin, the food the boys are gathering and words they do not understand. Only then does Granser's long account of the old society emerge. When rereading, distinguish three times: the author's early-twentieth-century world, Granser's pre-epidemic memories, and the boys' post-collapse present.

Before the plague: Granser teaches literature, large cities thrive and science studies microorganisms. In 2013: the Scarlet Plague spreads, students and officials die, communication and social order collapse, and a guarded refuge fails. Afterward: Granser crosses deserted country, lives alone in Yosemite, meets Vesta and Bill, and later joins the Santa Rosa survivors. In the story's later present: his descendants listen on the beach, and Edwin dreams of regaining weapons and power. Exact gaps or uncertain recollections should not be filled with invented dates.

Six chapters, six turning points

CHAPTER I

The beach and the old railway

The bear, the coin and the word «escarlata» expose the distance between Granser’s memories and the children’s world.

Reflect: What can an object preserve when its meaning has been forgotten?

CHAPTER II

The vanished world

Granser uses objects to explain enormous populations. His account of wealth and work raises questions about who was free.

CHAPTER III

The epidemic arrives

Early reassurance gives way to deaths, fear and lost communication. Hoffmeyer’s supposed discovery remains unconfirmed.

Reflect: Which claims are witnessed, reported or uncertain?

CHAPTER IV

The refuge fails

The guarded Chemistry Building offers water and shelter, but the disease is already among the refugees. Granser eventually survives alone.

Reflect: How do different people respond when protecting others becomes dangerous?

Return to the recording
CHAPTER V

Solitude and Vesta

Material safety in Yosemite does not end Granser’s loneliness. Meeting Vesta and Bill confronts him with violence and reversed social power.

Reflect: How do Granser’s class prejudices complicate his response to Bill?

CHAPTER VI

Knowledge and power

Family history links the survivors to the children. Granser hopes to preserve books, but recovered weapons may bring new domination.
