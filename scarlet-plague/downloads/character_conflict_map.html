Family relationships — Chapter VI

James Howard Smith («Granser») is the elderly storyteller and former professor. Edwin, Hoo-Hoo and Hare-Lip are children of the new world, each associated with a different tribe. Bertha later becomes Granser's wife. Their daughter Vera marries Sandow. Sandow is the son of Vesta Van Warden and Chauffeur Bill. Thus Hare-Lip, the son of Vera and Sandow, is linked to Granser and Bertha as well as to Vesta and Bill; Edwin and Hoo-Hoo descend from Granser and Bertha through other children. The late family explanation should be studied only after finishing Chapter VI.

Read the narrator critically

Granser remembers that the richest families controlled land, machines and political power, while many workers owned little. He criticizes coercion in the old social order but also retains pride in his former status. Vesta suffers violence and control under Bill. The reader need not endorse Granser's prejudice about a former servant gaining power in order to condemn Bill's behavior. The source's racial and class assumptions should likewise be discussed as part of its historical perspective, not repeated as scientific or ethical truth.

Your task: Describe Vesta in Spanish using what the story supports. Separate her actions and situation from Granser’s judgments.
