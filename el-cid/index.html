Pronunciation guide

Pronunciation model: modern north-central Peninsular Spanish, with /s/–/θ/ distinction and yeísmo (ll and consonantal y share /ʝ/). Other regional pronunciations are valid. IPA uses broad transcription between slashes, not narrow speech detail. The approximate English-friendly guide uses ah, eh, ee, oh, oo for the five steady Spanish vowels (no English-style diphthongs); y and w for glides; th as in thin; kh for the Spanish j sound (a back-of-mouth fricative); ny as in canyon; r for a brief tongue tap and rr for a trill. Say g as in go, s as in see; h in ah/eh/oh is not a separate sound. Capitals mark stressed syllables; in phrases, unstressed function words remain lowercase. Hyphens in the syllable line show written-word divisions, not connected-speech resyllabification. For words with two lexical stresses, such as adverbs ending in -mente, both are shown. Diphthongs and hiatuses may vary across legitimate accents. Methodological references: RAE–ASALE, Ortografía de la lengua española, sections on sounds and phonemes, consonantal subsystems, and principles of graphic accentuation; Diccionario panhispánico de dudas, entries diptongo and rehusar(se); I. Mackenzie, Castilian Spanish (Newcastle University). These references support the model; the transcriptions and guides are editorially prepared, not copied from a pronunciation dictionary.

sons-in-law; men married to one’s daughters

Definition, pronunciation & example

Sustantivo masculino plural; singular: yerno. Maridos de las hijas de una persona.

IPA: /ˈʝeɾnos/
Guide: YEHR-nohs
Syllables: YER-nos

Les anunció que traía yernos de una familia poderosa.

He announced that he was bringing sons-in-law from a powerful family.

RAE–ASALE, DLE, yerno, sense 1.
