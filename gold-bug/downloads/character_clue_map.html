Character & Clue Map

Spoiler warning: this page explains how characters and clues contribute to the solution.
Person / clueContribution
William LegrandNaturalist and analytical problem-solver. He notices the heat reaction, reconstructs the cipher, identifies the geographical instructions and calculates the excavation line.
The unnamed narratorSceptical observer. His interpretation changes from concern about Legrand's apparent irrationality to recognition of a coherent method.
JupiterLegrand's older Black companion, formerly enslaved and now free. He is essential to the expedition, climbs the tree and accidentally creates the left/right error that causes the first failed dig.
WolfLegrand's large Newfoundland dog. His jump helps expose the parchment to heat in Chapter 1, and later he paws at the corrected excavation where the old bones begin to appear.
Lieutenant G.Borrows the beetle. Because he places it directly in his pocket, Legrand keeps the parchment without noticing its importance.
The old womanRemembers the local place-name Bessop's Castle and shows Legrand the route to the rock formation.
Captain KiddThe kid/Kidd wordplay supports Legrand's hypothesis about the message's language and pirate connection. The story does not independently prove that Kidd buried this specific treasure.
The parchmentThe durable material carries the hidden skull, kid emblem and cryptogram; heat reveals what ordinary viewing cannot.
The skull in the treeProvides the left-eye drop point required by the decoded instructions.
The gold bugHas no supernatural power. Legrand uses it as the hanging object mainly to mystify the narrator; any suitable weight could have marked the drop point.
